If you use Proton, the reporting we read points to nothing you need to change in your account today. It describes how Proton builds software, not an incident. It reports no breach of user data, and none of it says Proton's encryption is weaker.

Links to Proton Mail and Proton Drive on switch-to.eu are affiliate links, and Proton is one of the site's recommendations.

Proton CEO Andy Yen apologised on 30 October for things he said to staff, a day after 404 Media [published an investigation](https://www.404media.co/manifestos-fear-and-loser-thinking-inside-protons-ai-reckoning/) into his push for AI-written code. In [a post on r/ProtonMail](https://www.reddit.com/r/ProtonMail/comments/1xjo8d4/a_message_to_the_community/), he also said AI will never come at the expense of security, and promised to publish how Proton reviews its code.

## What 404 Media reports

404 Media spoke to eight current and one former Proton employee and reviewed Slack chats, meeting recordings and an internal document. Its two articles are paywalled. We read their openings and [heise's summary](https://www.heise.de/news/Nachrichten-Leak-Wachstum-scheint-bei-Proton-aktuell-wichtiger-als-Sicherheit-11508413.html), not the full text.

According to messages 404 Media saw, an employee urged the company in late August to take a few days to security-test new code before release. Yen called that "loser thinking". He replied that speed and security go together, heise reports.

404 Media also reports that Yen told engineers to stop handcrafting code and use AI more. A meeting recording it holds puts AI's share of Proton's code in October at 90%, heise writes. One employee wrote that security had low priority at Proton and that AI wouldn't solve this.

A Proton spokesperson did not dispute the reporting, according to 404 Media, and said Proton has at times been too blunt or too slow in explaining its direction to staff.

## What Proton says

Yen's post says Proton added layers of security review in the past year, "contrary to what has been alleged", and uses AI to look for vulnerabilities in its own code.

He calls one report false. 404 Media wrote that Proton installed software on staff computers that tracks how much AI code the company generates. Yen blames a misunderstanding of device-management software. He also [cancelled](https://404media.co/protons-ceo-cancels-ai-re-interviews) a planned round of staff "re-interviews" about AI use.

Yen wrote that "the burden of proof about this is on us". He promised to publish how Proton does code and security reviews and where AI is allowed, and to open-source internal tools, including more advanced versions of [Lumo](/services/eu/proton-lumo). He gave no date.

CTO Bart Butler's [AI memo](https://proton.me/blog/ai-manifesto), published on Proton's blog on 1 November, says Proton "will get to a point where most human code review will end". It also says tests "remain crucial". His introduction says the memo doesn't go into the specifics of security.

## If you stay with Proton

The question still matters, even with nothing to change today. As heise writes, the best encryption doesn't help if attackers crack the apps themselves. Watch two things:

- The promised publication. Look for who checks code before release, and where people still review it.
- Any new independent audit. Proton's apps are open source and Securitum has audited them in the past.
