More than 35 builders of alternative social apps have asked the European Commission for a right to take your social network with you when you leave a platform. They signed a joint statement ahead of the Digital Fairness Act, the EU's planned update of online consumer law, [Rapporteur reports](https://www.rapporteur.com/news/eu-urged-to-ease-social-media-switching/). The Commission is due to present that proposal on 11 November.

The core idea: every social platform would keep each account in a "personal data server" built to one common standard. Any app you authorise would read and write to the same account, and you'd be able to move the server to another host without losing your network, according to [the coalition's position paper](https://digitalfreedomofmovement.eu/).

## Why your followers stay behind

Platforms already let you download your data. According to the coalition, a download isn't enough: the GDPR and the Digital Markets Act require platforms to hand over data, but not your connections in a form another service understands.

The coalition's own test from September 2026 shows the gap. Exporting from Instagram took 16 steps, from X 10 and from LinkedIn 9. X handed over followers as numeric IDs without usernames. In none of the three cases did followers move with the user.

The group also wants the EU to ban practices that stop people finding each other elsewhere, such as removing or suppressing links to your profiles on other services. Today's rules leave gaps here. The Digital Markets Act's interoperability rules don't cover every large social network, so X's ban on "account matching" tools falls outside EU enforcement, the signatories say.

## Who is asking

The Modal Foundation coordinates the paper. On 3 November its site listed 70 signatories, 45 of them builders, from 15 countries. Rapporteur named Sherif Elsayed-Ali of the alternative social network Eurosky and Robin Berjon of the IPFS Project among them.

One detail matters. The proposed standard starts from the personal data server of the AT Protocol, and a builder from the US company Bluesky PBC signed the paper. The coalition says apps on the AT Protocol already reach more than 45 million users.

The Commission hasn't presented its proposal yet. We won't know whether the Digital Fairness Act says anything about followers or connections until then.

## What this means for you

For now, leaving a big platform still means rebuilding your network by hand.

If you want to start somewhere else today, [Mastodon](/services/eu/mastodon) is the EU-based option in our [social media list](/services/social-media). A German nonprofit builds the software, and thousands of independent servers connect through the ActivityPub protocol. The trade-off is a smaller audience: about 800,000 to 1 million monthly active users.

[Bluesky](/services/eu/bluesky) runs on the AT Protocol the coalition wants as the starting point. The company is based in Seattle and stores your data in the United States. Moving from X?
