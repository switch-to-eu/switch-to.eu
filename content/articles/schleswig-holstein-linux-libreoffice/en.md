The northern German state of Schleswig-Holstein [announced in April 2024](https://itwire.com/business-it-news/open-source/german-state-to-move-30,000-pcs-from-windows-office-to-linux-libreoffice.html) that it will move its administration away from Microsoft. Around 30,000 workstations are set to switch from Microsoft Office to LibreOffice, and later from Windows to Linux.

The state's digitalisation minister, Dirk Schrödter, presented it as a matter of digital sovereignty: control over the software, the data and the costs, instead of depending on a single US supplier.

## Step by step

The plan is staged. Office software comes first, followed by email and calendars, and the operating system last. Email is already done: by October 2025 the state had [moved about 44,000 mailboxes](https://www.boerse-express.com/news/articles/open-source-wende-30000-mitarbeiter-in-schleswig-holstein-umgestiegen-921880) from Microsoft Exchange to Open-Xchange and Thunderbird.

Worth knowing: the city of Munich tried a similar move in the 2000s with its LiMux project and later returned to Windows, citing compatibility problems and user complaints. Schleswig-Holstein's staged approach is partly a response to that history.

## What this means for you

A state can take years to switch. You can start in an afternoon. LibreOffice is free on every desktop platform, and [Nextcloud](/services/eu/nextcloud) gives you shared files and calendars on EU servers.
