The International Criminal Court in The Hague is moving its staff off Microsoft Office and onto openDesk. The German business paper Handelsblatt [reported the plan](https://heise.de/-10964189) in October 2025, covering about 1,800 workstations, and the court confirmed it on 31 October.

openDesk is an open source workplace suite developed for the German public sector and coordinated by ZenDiS, the German government's Centre for Digital Sovereignty. It combines existing open source tools, including Nextcloud for files, Collabora for documents and Element for chat, into one package.

## Why it matters

The move follows months of concern about the court's dependence on a US vendor. Earlier in 2025 the US government sanctioned the court's chief prosecutor, and reports that he lost access to his Microsoft email account raised the question of whether a US company could be forced to cut off an international institution. Microsoft president Brad Smith has [denied](https://www.ejiltalk.org/justice-recoded-why-it-matters-that-the-international-criminal-court-embraced-open-source-software-and-ditched-microsoft/) that it suspended the court's services.

Whatever happened in that case, the risk is structural. An organisation that relies on one foreign provider for email and documents can be switched off by decisions it doesn't control.

## What this means for you

The parts of openDesk are available on their own. [Nextcloud](/services/eu/nextcloud) handles files and calendars, and [Element](/services/eu/element) is a chat app built on the open Matrix standard, which the French and German governments also use.
