### Will the people you talk to come along?

A messenger only works if your contacts use the same app. Signal costs nothing, so nobody you invite has to pay.

Threema costs €6, once. That's a small amount, but everyone you invite pays the same. Session and Element are free. Session gives everyone a long random ID instead of a phone number, and Element asks you to pick a server first, so expect to explain a little more.

### Do you want to keep your phone number to yourself?

Signal needs a phone number to create an account. By default, only people who already have your number in their contacts see your number. Everyone else connects through a username you share.

Threema and Session give you a random ID and need no phone number. Session doesn't ask for an email address either. Your messages travel through a decentralised network of over 1,500 nodes, which Session says hides your IP address.

Element asks for a username on the Matrix server you pick. An email address is optional.

### Where are the companies and their servers?

None of the messengers on this page has its head office in an EU member state. Threema AG is in Pfäffikon, Switzerland, and the German investment firm Comitis Capital has owned Threema since 2026. A Swiss foundation runs Session. Element's head office is in London. Signal is a US nonprofit in Mountain View, California.

The servers differ too. Threema says all its servers are in Switzerland. Signal's terms say your encrypted data and metadata go to the United States and other countries. Session runs on its network of nodes instead of company servers. On Element, your account lives on the Matrix server you choose, or on your own.

All four say they encrypt your messages end to end, so whoever runs the servers has no way to read them.

### How big are your groups and calls?

Signal groups hold up to 1,000 people, and group calls up to 75. Threema groups hold up to 256 people, with group calls up to 16.

Session groups stop at 100 people. Its voice and video calls are a beta feature, switched off until you turn them on in the settings. Element's free app includes 1:1 and group voice and video calls.

### Do you want to run your own server?

Element is the only app here built for that. Element runs on Matrix, an open standard where servers talk to each other. You host your own server and still chat with people on matrix.org or any other Matrix server.

Element's pricing page lists a free Community edition for up to 100 users, meant for non-professional use and testing. Organisations with 100 users or more pay per seat, and the page doesn't list that price. Running a server takes technical know-how. If you don't have that know-how, a free account on an existing server is the easier start.
