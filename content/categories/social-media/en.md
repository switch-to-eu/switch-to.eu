### Where is the company, and where does your data sit?

Only one network on this page has its head office in an EU member state: Mastodon, from Germany. Bluesky Social is an American public benefit corporation. We list Bluesky because the code is open source and you take your account to another host whenever you want.

Mastodon is a network of thousands of independent servers. An organisation or a person runs each one, with its own rules. Your data sits with the server you join. The server list on joinmastodon.org filters by region, so you find servers based in Europe. Then check the country: a server run from an EU member state falls under the GDPR.

Bluesky's privacy policy allows the company to store your data anywhere in the world. Bluesky names a representative for EU users in Cork, Ireland.

### What do you want from your feed?

Mastodon shows posts from the people you follow in chronological order. No algorithm picks posts for you, and the Mastodon team says they will never serve ads.

Bluesky lets you choose. Anyone builds a feed, and Bluesky counts over 40,000 of them, each with its own rules for what you see.

The audience differs too. Bluesky reported over 43 million users in March 2026. Mastodon's audience is smaller and spread over thousands of servers.

### Do you want to keep your followers when you move?

On Mastodon, you link your old account to an account on a new server, and your followers move over automatically. Your posts stay behind. After a move, you wait 30 days before the next one.

Bluesky says you change servers without losing your username, friends or posts. The AT Protocol's migration guide does this with API calls and points to tools such as goat and PDS MOOver. Expect a technical job.

### Are your private messages private?

Not on either network. Mastodon's documentation says the software is not an encrypted messaging app, and warns that the admins of both servers might see the text of a private mention. Bluesky's privacy policy says direct messages are unencrypted and open to its Trust & Safety team.

Keep private conversations in a messenger with end-to-end encryption, where only you and the other person read the messages.
