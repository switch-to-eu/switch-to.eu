### Do you need live traffic?

Google Maps steers you around traffic jams. Of the four apps here, HERE WeGo comes closest at no cost: arrival times take live traffic into account. The catch: HERE only switches on its traffic features if you share your location, speed and direction with the company. Unless you limit this in the settings, the app also collects them while closed, when HERE detects that you're driving.

Magic Earth puts traffic-aware routing in Premium, at €14.99 a year in the App Store. Organic Maps has no live traffic. In the project's own poll of nearly 1,000 users at the end of 2025, traffic information came second among the most wanted features. OsmAnd's pages don't list live traffic.

### Do you travel by public transport?

HERE WeGo covers public transport in more than 1,900 cities. Magic Earth says the app shows transit routes with schedules in hundreds of regions. Organic Maps added routes by bus, tram, subway and light rail in June 2026, without live departure times so far. OsmAnd's public transport routing is still in testing: you see the route, but get no turn-by-turn directions.

### Will you use the app without mobile data?

Downloaded maps save mobile data, and roaming costs abroad. Organic Maps is free and every feature works offline. HERE WeGo lets you download a whole region, country or continent at no cost, except in Japan, Korea and China.

OsmAnd's free version stops at 7 map downloads on Android and 5 on iPhone, and each update counts as a download. Maps+ removes the limit for €14.99 a year or €69.99 once. Magic Earth offers offline maps only with Premium.

### How much do you want to share?

Organic Maps says the app collects no data and needs no registration. OsmAnd's privacy policy says the company collects no personal information without your consent, and you only need an account for its cloud backup. Magic Earth says the company doesn't track your movements.

HERE's privacy notice says the company links your location data to random identifiers that don't identify you. The same notice allows location-based ads in the app. Organic Maps and OsmAnd publish their source code for anyone to check.

### Who makes the app, and where does the map come from?

All four apps come from companies with their head office in an EU member state. Organic Maps OÜ is in Tallinn, Estonia. In 2025 some of its contributors copied the code into a separate app, CoMaps, after a dispute over governance and financial transparency. OsmAnd B.V. is in Amstelveen, and Magic Lane, the maker of Magic Earth, in Amsterdam. HERE Technologies is based in the Netherlands, and the shareholders HERE listed in 2020 include Audi, BMW, Mercedes-Benz and Intel.

Organic Maps, OsmAnd and Magic Earth use OpenStreetMap, a map built by volunteers. HERE uses its own map data. Before you switch, search for a few places you visit often and check their opening hours.
