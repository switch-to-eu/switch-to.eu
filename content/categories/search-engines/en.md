### Who sees your searches?

All three say they don't build a profile of you. The difference is what they pass on to Google or Microsoft, whose results they use.

Ecosia's privacy policy says each search sends your IP address and search terms to Bing or Google. On its own servers, Ecosia anonymises IP addresses after 7 days at most. Qwant says Microsoft gets your search terms and a shortened IP address. Qwant's policy also allows the full address to go to Microsoft for spam and click-fraud checks. Startpage says your search goes out without your IP address, and that the company keeps no log of what you search.

### Where do the results come from?

None of the three runs fully on its own index. Startpage gets results from Google, Bing and other providers. Where Google results are available to you, Ecosia shows them by default, and you switch to Bing in Ecosia's settings. Elsewhere you get Bing. Qwant crawls the web with its own robots and fills the gaps with Bing.

Qwant and Ecosia also own EUSP together, a European search index based in Paris. Ecosia says EUSP results are served only in France.

In practice, if you want results close to Google's, Startpage and Ecosia both draw on Google.

### Does your browser list the engine?

Safari lists Ecosia on iPhone and Mac, according to Ecosia's help pages. Qwant says Safari doesn't include Qwant, and offers a Qwant for Safari extension on the Mac. Startpage says Safari users have no way to make Startpage their default search engine.

In Firefox, Chrome and Edge, the list depends on your country. Ecosia says Firefox offers Ecosia in eight countries, among them Germany, Belgium and the Netherlands. If an engine is missing in Chrome or Edge, search on its website once, then check the list again.

### Who owns them, and how do they make money?

All three have their head office in an EU member state: Qwant in Paris, Ecosia in Berlin and Startpage in The Hague. Startpage says the company is part of System1, a publicly traded company based in the United States.

Ecosia says its shares have not been for sale at a profit since 2018, and no owner takes profits out of the company. All of its profits go to climate action, mainly tree planting.

All three are free and show ads next to your results. By default, those ads follow your search terms, not a profile. Ecosia and Qwant show personalised ads only if you agree to them.
