Proton Mail is an encrypted email service based in Geneva, Switzerland. Scientists who met at CERN founded it in 2014, and Proton passed 100 million accounts across its services in 2023. The company behind it, Proton AG, is overseen by the Proton Foundation, a Swiss non-profit and its largest shareholder. There are no venture capital investors. Proton says its paid plans fund the company and that it doesn't sell your data.

Only you can read your emails. Proton stores your mailbox encrypted and says even its own team can't read your messages. Emails between Proton users are end-to-end encrypted automatically. Mail from other providers gets encrypted as soon as it reaches Proton's servers. For emails to outside providers, you can send password-protected messages that expire after a set time.

Every account, the free one included, comes with basic versions of Proton's VPN, cloud storage, calendar and password manager. Proton Unlimited gives you the paid version of each. All of Proton's apps are open source, so anyone can inspect the code. Servers sit in Switzerland, Germany, and Norway.

## Worth knowing

The free plan starts with 500 MB of storage, which grows to 1 GB once you finish a few setup steps. It allows 150 messages per day. That's tight if you get a lot of email. Mail Plus costs €3.99 per month on a yearly plan.

Proton is a Swiss company, not an EU one. Switzerland has strong privacy laws, but it's not part of the EU. When Swiss courts issue valid legal orders, Proton must comply. This has happened several times with activist accounts, where Proton handed over metadata like IP addresses and payment information. Your email content stays encrypted and unreadable, but metadata is not covered by that protection. Subject lines aren't end-to-end encrypted either.

Some users report slow support replies, especially on the free plan. Paid plans include priority support. A few users also report account suspensions with little explanation.
