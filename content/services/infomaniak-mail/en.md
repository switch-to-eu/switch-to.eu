Infomaniak Mail is the email service of Infomaniak, a Geneva company that has run its own data centers since 1994. If you have seen an address ending in @ik.me, it comes from here: ik.me, etik.com and ikmail.com are the free domains of my kSuite, Infomaniak's free personal plan. Infomaniak says it keeps your mailbox in two of its own data centers, both in Switzerland.

Infomaniak says it grew without outside capital. Since 1 October 2026 its shares trade on the SIX Swiss Exchange, and a public-benefit foundation holds most of the voting rights.

The free plan gives you one address, 20 GB for email and 15 GB of kDrive cloud storage. my kSuite+ costs €19 a year and adds unlimited email storage, 1 TB of cloud storage, email forwarding and AI that writes and translates inside Mail. Infomaniak says its AI runs in Switzerland and doesn't store the emails it processes. You also get a calendar, contacts and kMeet video calls.

Want an address on your own domain? The Starter plan gives you one free address when you buy a domain name. Plans with more addresses start at €1.58 per user a month.

Switching over is not complicated. Infomaniak copies your old emails and folders to your new address, and offers to move your addresses for you at no charge. Your mail works in the webmail, the mobile apps and any email app that supports IMAP.

## Worth knowing

Encryption works differently from Proton Mail or Tuta. In Infomaniak Mail you encrypt an email with one click when you send it. Other Infomaniak users open it automatically, Gmail or Outlook users with a password you send them separately. Infomaniak says it keeps the encryption keys in its own data centers and unlocks them when you log in. If you want a provider that says it has no way to read your emails, look at Proton Mail or Tuta.

Infomaniak's price pages showed Swiss francs, without VAT. It bills in euros or Swiss francs, depending on the country your account is registered in. And while the company is based in Switzerland (not the EU), it says it complies with both Swiss and EU privacy law.
