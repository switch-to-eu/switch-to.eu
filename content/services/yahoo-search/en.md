  ## Company Overview

  Yahoo Search is one of the oldest search engines, originally founded in 1994. In September 2021, Apollo Global Management acquired 90% of Yahoo from Verizon for roughly $5 billion. Verizon retained a 10% stake. Jim Lanzone serves as CEO. Headquarters moved to New York. Employee count is approximately 4,000-5,000. Yahoo Search is powered by Microsoft Bing for core search results, with Yahoo adding its own features including Yahoo Scout (AI-powered answers).

  ## Data Collection Practices

  Yahoo collects extensive data: IP addresses, search queries, browsing behavior, location data, device information (browser version, OS, mobile network info), cookie data, and advertising identifiers. Uses cookies, beacons, web storage, SDKs, tags, and pixels for tracking. Search user log data is de-identified within 18 months, IP addresses within 6 months. A 2025 class action lawsuit alleges Yahoo's ConnectID system (released 2020) creates email-based identifiers to track users across websites and apps without disclosure, building profiles on 300+ million users even when cookies are cleared or private browsing is used.

  ## GDPR and Privacy Enforcement

  December 2023: CNIL fined Yahoo 10 million euros for placing ~20 tracking cookies despite users rejecting them, and for coercing Yahoo Mail users who withdrew consent by threatening loss of access. December 2022: Microsoft (Bing, which powers Yahoo Search) fined 60 million euros by CNIL for cookie consent violations. Yahoo transfers EU/EEA/UK user data to the United States. Subject to US surveillance laws including FISA Section 702.

  ## Historical Security Incidents

  2013: All 3 billion Yahoo user accounts breached (largest data breach in history). 2014: 500 million accounts breached by Russian state-sponsored hackers. Both breaches were disclosed only in late 2016. Exposed data included names, email addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, hashed passwords, and security questions. Yahoo paid $117.5 million in class-action settlement and $35 million SEC fine for delayed disclosure.

Yahoo Search launched in 1994 and changed hands several times before Apollo Global Management bought it from Verizon in 2021 for about $5 billion. The search results come from Microsoft Bing. Yahoo layers its own features on top: Yahoo Scout for AI-generated answers, plus integrated news, finance, and sports.

Every search gets logged. Yahoo collects your IP address, search queries, browsing behavior, location, and device details. Cookies, tracking pixels, and beacons feed into advertising profiles. Search data stays on file for up to 18 months before anonymization. IP addresses stick around for 6 months.

Since Yahoo relies on Bing, your queries pass through two US companies before returning results. Both have been fined by French regulators for cookie consent violations. All data sits on US servers, subject to US surveillance laws.

## Worth knowing

Yahoo holds the record for the largest data breaches in history. Attacks in 2013 and 2014 exposed all 3 billion user accounts, but the company only told users in late 2016. That delay cost Yahoo $117.5 million in settlements and a $35 million SEC fine. More recently, a 2025 class action lawsuit claims Yahoo's ConnectID system tracks users across websites through email-based identifiers that work even in private browsing mode.
