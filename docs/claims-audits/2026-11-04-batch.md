## gdprCompliance

**What it claims.** The site renders `compliant` as a flat, unattributed "GDPR Compliant" (`services/eu/[service_name]/page.tsx`, `lib/services.ts`, `lib/llm-content.ts`). It reads as a finding.

**How other listings use it.** 28 compliant, 16 partial, 3 non-compliant (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok), 0 unknown. `content-files.md` gives only the enum and `/research` says "GDPR posture". No criteria are written down. In practice EU and recommended services get `compliant`, including Mistral, which trains on chats by default. US-based services get `partial`, such as Claude, where the note cites Standard Contractual Clauses.

**Euria.** Rests on Infomaniak's own statements. No audit or regulator decision on the pages read. A Swiss company, so this is a claim about GDPR rules applied from outside the EU. `gdprNotes` attributes it correctly; the label doesn't. Supported only as "the company says so".

**Ecosia AI Chat.** Rests on Ecosia's own statement. Same gap, plus points that a reader might weigh: OpenAI fallback and Cloudflare with US transfers under standard contractual clauses, an email opt-out alongside "may request your consent", and a help FAQ saying chat data isn't shared with third parties while the policy sends it to Mistral. Claude gets `partial` with SCC transfers, though there the US is the main route, not a fallback.

**Search.** Two searches found no fine or complaint against either company. That is not evidence of compliance.

**Suggestion.** Neither value is contradicted, and neither is proven. Options for the owner: define `compliant` as "the company states it and nothing we read contradicts it" and label it "Says it follows GDPR", across all listings, or set both to `unknown`, which hides the label. `claims-audit` already treats a self-declared "GDPR compliant" in `certifications` as overclaimed.

## openSource

**Euria: `true`.** Published: iOS and Android apps, GPL-3.0 (`ios-euria`, `android-euria`, both active), and the system prompt (`euria-prompt`, no licence file). Not found among Infomaniak's 169 public repos: Euria's server or web app. Forks of LibreChat (MIT) and vLLM (Apache-2.0) exist, but nothing read links them to Euria. Models: third-party open models, not Infomaniak's, and the chat model isn't named, so their licences can't be checked. The value holds for the mobile apps only.

**Ecosia AI Chat: field absent.** No chat code found in Ecosia's org. The Mistral models it uses aren't named, nor are the open-weight ones. `ios-browser` (MPL-2.0) belongs to the browser. Absent is the right value. Don't set `true`.

**Mistral comparison.** `content/services/mistral-le-chat/en.md`: `openSource: true`, `sourceCodeUrl: https://github.com/mistralai`. researchNotes says the flag rests on open weights and that the pages read don't say the web and mobile apps are open source. Owner decision, not re-opened here. Result: the same "Yes" means weights for Mistral and client apps for Euria, and the table doesn't say which.

**Side note, outside this audit.** `ecosia-browser` has `openSource: false` while `ecosia/ios-browser` is public under MPL-2.0.
