# Top pages: what to change to rank and keep visitors

## Summary

The site got 142 clicks from 13,664 impressions in 90 days. Most of the missed clicks sit on four pages that already rank on page one or the top of page two: the WhatsApp to Signal guide, the Gmail to Proton Mail guide, the X to Bluesky guide and the browsers category. All four guides run on default metadata ("Migrating from X | switch-to.eu" and a 54 to 66 character description), and none of them answers the questions people search with: stickers and groups for Signal, storage limits for Proton Mail, the Porto extension for Bluesky.

The second problem is on-site. The two biggest category pages after the homepage, storage (111 visitors) and browsers (80 visitors), hold 60 and 75 words of generic text above a card grid. Visitors spend 21 to 91 seconds and 84 to 92% leave. These visitors come from inside the site, so this is a retention problem first and a search problem second.

The owner's question, "should we push Proton Drive on the storage page?", gets a no in that form. Turn the storage page into a short buyer's guide where Proton Drive is one of four "best for" picks, with its reason, its trade-offs and a plain affiliate note on the same card. Fix Proton Drive's prices first: four of its five paid tiers are wrong. Details in [Storage and Proton Drive](#storage-and-proton-drive-the-owners-question).

## How to read the numbers

- **Query matching is by topic.** The export has no page-by-query pairs. Queries were matched to pages by subject (a sticker query goes to the Signal guide). The query export covers 4,721 of 13,664 impressions (35%). The rest is anonymised long tail.
- **Expected impact** is extra clicks per 90 days. The low end assumes the page reaches an average CTR for its current position. The high end assumes it also moves up to the position named in the table. The CTR curve is an assumption (roughly 5% at position 5, 3% at 7, 2% at 9, 1% at 12, 0.7% at 16). Treat the numbers as ranking, not forecast.
- **Bounce includes vendor clicks.** A visitor who clicks "Try Proton Drive" counts as a bounce. On pages with a vendor button, high bounce can mean success. Plausible is installed (`apps/website/app/(frontend)/[locale]/layout.tsx`) but outbound clicks are not tracked. Turn on outbound link tracking before judging any bounce rate in this report.
- **Context.** Impressions fell from 5,731 (August) to 3,316 (October) while average position improved from 16.9 to 14.5. The United States brings 1,973 impressions at 0.25% CTR. Belgium (9.7%) and France (3.3%) click far more. Desktop has 69% of impressions at position 16.6 and 0.78% CTR. Mobile ranks better (11.5) and clicks twice as often (1.63%).

## Storage and Proton Drive: the owner's question

**Question:** the storage page gets many visitors, so should we push Proton Drive?

**Answer: not as a bigger push.** Make the storage page a short buyer's guide where Proton Drive keeps a featured spot for a stated reason, next to three other "best for" picks, with its trade-offs and a plain affiliate note on the same card. Fix Proton Drive's facts before giving it more prominence.

### What the data says

1. **The visitors come from inside the site, not from search.** 111 visitors in 90 days, but Search Console gives the page 0 clicks (94 impressions, position 48). The homepage (807 visitors) already shows Proton Drive as the storage pick, because featured services sort first in `fetchHomepagePicks` (`apps/website/app/(frontend)/[locale]/page.tsx`). Pushing Proton Drive harder on the storage page reaches people who saw it one click earlier.
2. **Proton Drive already gets the largest share.** Visitors to storage pages in the same 90 days (not all of them came through the category page): Proton Drive 37, Google Drive to pCloud guide 27, pCloud 16, Nextcloud 10, Tresorit 2. Proton Drive is featured on the storage page, the homepage and the Google Drive page (`recommendedAlternative: proton-drive`).
3. **The page doesn't help anyone choose.** 60 words, 91 seconds, 55% scroll. Visitors read the featured card and the top of the grid, then leave. Part of the 84% bounce could be clicks to Proton, but outbound clicks aren't tracked, so nobody can tell. Turn on outbound tracking first.
4. **Search demand around storage leans to pCloud and generic queries.** pCloud: 800 impressions, 5 clicks. Google Drive to pCloud guide: 606 impressions (EN plus NL), 4 clicks. Proton Drive: 748 impressions, 0 clicks, mostly brand queries proton.me wins. Generic queries ("european cloud storage", "online backup europe") rank at 23 to 66 because the category page has nothing to rank with.
5. **The Proton Drive listing has wrong prices** in four of five paid tiers, and the free plan is overstated (see section 7). Promoting it now puts those errors in front of more people.
6. **The brand rules point the same way.** A featured affiliate pick with no stated reason on a 60-word page reads like a paid placement. The live card's note says "This link may be an affiliate link" when it is one, and the card shows "Premium: Free (5 GB)" because of a label bug.

### What to build: a short buyer's guide

Target 600 to 900 words, written with `/write-category storage`, then `/humanize-category` and `/seo-check-category`. Structure:

1. **Two-sentence intro.** What "European" means here. Three of the four services are Swiss: Switzerland is outside the EU but has an EU adequacy decision. Nextcloud is German.
2. **Best-for picks**, each with its reason and its trade-off on the same card. Draft wording below; verify every fact first.
   - **Proton Drive: best if you want every file encrypted on your device by default, or already pay for Proton Mail.** Worth knowing: it costs more per TB than pCloud, the free plan starts at 2 GB, Linux has no native app (as of early 2026, check), and bulk uploads run slower because of encryption. *Affiliate link: we earn a commission if you subscribe to Proton through it. pCloud, Tresorit and Nextcloud pay us nothing. The order here is by use case, not commission.*
   - **pCloud: best for large photo and video libraries, and for paying once with a lifetime plan.** Worth knowing: choose the EU region (Luxembourg) at sign-up because changing it later costs money, on-device encryption is a paid add-on, and the apps are not open source.
   - **Tresorit: best for teams in regulated work such as law or health.** Worth knowing: no free plan, from $11.99/month, and a lost password means lost files.
   - **Nextcloud: best if you want to run the server yourself, or need files, chat and office in one place.** Worth knowing: self-hosting needs Linux skills, and hosted plans vary by provider.
3. **Comparison table.** Company country, where files are stored, free storage, cheapest paid plan (dated "as of November 2026"), encrypted on your device by default, open source, Linux app.
4. **"How we pick"**, three lines: who pays us (Proton only), how picks are chosen, when we last checked prices.
5. **"Common questions"**: Is Swiss storage as safe as EU storage? Which one is cheapest per TB? How do I move from Google Drive?
6. **Fill the gaps later.** Competitors list Internxt (Spain), Filen (Germany), Koofr (Slovenia), Jottacloud (Norway) and Icedrive. None of our four consumer picks is based in an EU member state except self-hosted Nextcloud. Research Internxt, Filen, Koofr and Infomaniak kDrive before adding them.

**Competitor check.** For "european cloud storage" the top results are affiliate-driven lists. Internxt's blog lists 8 providers with an FAQ and puts itself first. Cloudwards' "Best Cloud Storage for Europe in 2026" has a quick-comparison table (free storage, best price, best for, standout feature, limitation) and affiliate links on every pick. Our edge is the same table with a real limitation column and no pay-for-rank.

**Template change needed.** The category body renders in the hero's right column (`services/[category]/page.tsx`). Render the first paragraph in the hero and the rest in a section below the card grid, or add a separate body field for long-form text. The same change serves the browsers page.
