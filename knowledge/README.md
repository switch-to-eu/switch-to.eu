# Knowledge base

The research memory behind switch-to.eu news. AI skills read and maintain it, a script keeps its indexes, and a human reviews every change in a PR. Design rationale: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-02-news-knowledge-base-design.md`.

**Scope:** EU digital sovereignty, privacy and the tech market. Everything here is written in **English**, whatever language the source is in. Quotes from non-English sources are translated, with the original language noted.

**Rule zero:** code does the bookkeeping, the LLM does the thinking. Never hand-edit `index.md` or `graph.json`. Run `pnpm knowledge:index` after every change. It rebuilds both, computes each story's `heat` (recent source activity) and reports broken links, orphans and stale pages. `pnpm knowledge:index --check` exits non-zero on broken links or invalid frontmatter.

## Layout

```
knowledge/
  README.md        # this file: the schema every skill follows
  watchlist.md     # what /news-scan monitors: publishers, feeds, queries, subreddits
  index.md         # GENERATED by pnpm knowledge:index
  graph.json       # GENERATED: nodes, edges, backlinks, story heat
  log.md           # append-only operation log
  flags.md         # services whose listing may be outdated because of news
  inbox/YYYY-MM-DD.md                    # daily scan output
  sources/YYYY/MM/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md   # one per thing we read
  entities/<kind>/<slug>.md              # companies, regulations, institutions, people, places
  stories/<slug>.md                      # ongoing storylines
```

Site content in `content/` (services, guides, categories, articles) is also part of the graph. Knowledge pages point at it by slug and never copy it.

## Slugs and links

- Slugs are lowercase kebab-case ASCII: `microsoft`, `cloud-act`, `dutch-government-cloud-exit`. A slug is unique across the whole knowledge base (sources, entities and stories share one namespace), so `[[slug]]` is never ambiguous. Source slugs always start with their date.
- Link in prose with wikilinks: `[[microsoft]]`, `[[cloud-act]]`, `[[2026-11-02-dutch-cloud-tender]]`, a claim with `[[2026-11-02-dutch-cloud-tender#c2]]`, an article with `[[articles/<slug>]]`, a service with `[[services/<slug>]]`.
- Structured edges go in frontmatter (`entities:`, `stories:`, `related:`, `services:`, `categories:`). The index script reads both.
- Before creating an entity, search for it, including aliases: `rg -il "aliases:.*\bmsft\b|^name: microsoft" knowledge/entities`. Duplicates are the main way this base rots.

## Node types

### Source: `sources/YYYY/MM/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md`

What we read. The date in the filename is the **fetch** date. Immutable after ingest: fix typos, never rewrite claims. If a source turns out wrong, record that on the entity or story page.

```markdown
---
type: source
title: Finance ministry tenders €120M sovereign cloud contract
url: https://example.org/article
publisher: NOS
published: '2026-10-30'
fetched: '2026-11-02'
lang: nl                  # language of the original
reliability: high         # high | medium | low
entities: [dutch-government, microsoft]
stories: [dutch-government-cloud-exit]
---
**Summary:** Two or three sentences, in English.

**Claims**
- `c1` The Ministry of Finance plans to move 40% of its workloads off Azure by 2028.
- `c2` The tender is worth €120M over four years.

**Quotes**
> "We cannot guarantee data stays out of reach of US law." (state secretary, translated from Dutch)
```

- **Reliability:** `high` for primary sources (official documents, court rulings, company filings, regulators) and established newsrooms. `medium` for trade press, company blogs and press releases about the company itself. `low` for forums, Reddit, social posts and anonymous sourcing. Low sources can raise a question, never settle one.
- **Claims** are atomic, one fact per line, numbered `c1`, `c2`… in order. Only what the source actually says. Attribute contested claims ("X says…").
- Keep 3 to 10 claims. Skip anything irrelevant to EU users or the market.

### Entity: `entities/<kind>/<slug>.md`

`kind` is one of `company`, `regulation`, `institution`, `person`, `place`. Products we list are not entities: link `services/<slug>` instead, and attach them to the company entity with `services:`.

```markdown
---
type: entity
kind: company
name: Microsoft
aliases: [MSFT, Microsoft Corporation]
services: [microsoft-edge]
related:
  - {to: cloud-act, rel: subject-to}
  - {to: openai, rel: invests-in}
summary: US cloud and software vendor, central to most EU sovereignty stories.
updated: '2026-11-02'
---
## Current state
Short, present tense, cited. Rewritten whenever new facts change it.

## Facts
- 2026-06-10 · Admitted it cannot guarantee EU data stays out of reach of US authorities. [[2026-06-10-french-senate-hearing#c1]]
- ~~2025-02-01 · Says its EU Data Boundary keeps all EU customer data in the EU.~~ superseded 2026-06-10 [[2025-02-01-eu-data-boundary#c1]]

## History
Superseded facts older than 12 months, one line each (moved here by /knowledge-prune).
```

- **Fact lines:** `- YYYY-MM-DD · statement. [[source#cN]]`. The date is when it happened, not when we read it. Newest first. Every fact cites at least one claim.
- **Contradiction:** never delete a fact. Strike it through, add `superseded YYYY-MM-DD`, and add the new fact above it.
- **`rel` values:** `owns`, `owned-by`, `subsidiary-of`, `invests-in`, `competes-with`, `partners-with`, `regulates`, `regulated-by`, `subject-to`, `enforces`, `member-of`, `based-in`, `leads`, `works-for`, `sued-by`, `fined-by`. Add a new one only if none fits, and add it to this list in the same PR.

### Story: `stories/<slug>.md`

An ongoing storyline. The main unit for news. Each article advances one story.

```markdown
---
type: story
title: Dutch government moves off US cloud
status: active            # active | dormant | closed
entities: [dutch-government, microsoft, cloud-act]
services: [microsoft-365]
categories: [storage, email]
started: '2025-03-14'
last_event: '2026-10-30'
last_covered: '2026-09-12'
updated: '2026-11-02'
---
## Summary
What the story is and where it stands now. One to three paragraphs, cited. Rewritten, not appended.

## Background
Older timeline events folded into prose by /knowledge-prune.

## Timeline
- 2026-10-30 · Finance ministry publishes tender. [[2026-11-02-dutch-cloud-tender#c1]]

## Open questions
- Which providers bid? Expected Q1 2027.

## Our coverage
- 2026-09-12 · [[articles/dutch-parliament-cloud-motion]], angle: the parliamentary vote.
```

- **Status:** `active` = an event in the last 90 days. `dormant` = no event for 90 days. `closed` = resolved, or no event for 365 days. Ingest re-activates a dormant story when something happens.
- **Timeline:** newest first. Past 25 entries, /knowledge-prune folds the oldest into `## Background`.
- **Our coverage** is how the writer avoids repeating itself. /news-write adds a line and sets `last_covered` when it writes a draft, so tomorrow's pick doesn't choose the same story. If a draft is rejected and deleted, /knowledge-lint removes the line and restores the previous `last_covered`.

### flags.md

When ingest records a fact that changes something a service listing states (price, ownership, data location, a breach, shutdown, new EU hosting), append a line:

```markdown
- 2026-11-02 · services/proton-mail · price increase for Mail Plus · [[2026-11-02-proton-pricing#c1]]
```

`/research` takes flagged services first and removes the line once the listing is updated.

### log.md

Append-only, one line per operation, greppable:

```
2026-11-02 scan    | 34 candidates, 9 kept | inbox/2026-11-02.md
2026-11-02 ingest  | sources/2026/11/2026-11-02-dutch-cloud-tender.md | +1 source, ~3 entities, ~1 story
2026-11-02 pick    | dutch-government-cloud-exit | angle: tender details
2026-11-02 write   | content/articles/dutch-finance-ministry-cloud-tender/en.md
2026-11-07 lint    | 2 duplicates merged, 4 stale summaries
```

## Writing style for knowledge pages

Plain, neutral, factual, English. Not the site's voice: these are notes, not copy. No speculation outside `## Open questions`. Attribute opinions. Use numbers and dates.
