## Summary
The European Commission is due to propose the Digital Fairness Act, its revamp of online consumer law, on 11 November 2026, according to Rapporteur. [[2026-11-03-euractiv-dfa-social-media-switching#c5]] Ahead of it, a coalition of alternative social app builders, coordinated by the Modal Foundation, asked for a right to take your social network with you when you leave a platform. [[2026-11-03-euractiv-dfa-social-media-switching#c1]] [[2026-11-03-dfom-joint-position-paper#c1]] Their proposal: every platform keeps each account in a "personal data server" built to one common standard, starting from the AT Protocol's, so any authorised app can use the same account and followers move with the user. [[2026-11-03-dfom-joint-position-paper#c4]] [[2026-11-03-dfom-joint-position-paper#c5]]

The coalition says today's exports from LinkedIn, Instagram and X don't let followers move. [[2026-11-03-dfom-joint-position-paper#c3]] It also wants suppression of links to rival profiles banned under the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, and stronger use of the Digital Markets Act's data portability rule, which doesn't cover every large social platform. [[2026-11-03-dfom-joint-position-paper#c7]] [[2026-11-03-dfom-joint-position-paper#c8]] [[2026-11-03-euractiv-dfa-social-media-switching#c8]]

## Open questions
- Will the Commission's proposal, due 11 November 2026, include any right to move followers or connections between platforms?
- Will Annex I of the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive be changed to ban suppressing links to profiles on other services?
- Will the Commission act on the Digital Markets Act Article 6(9) and 6(5) requests without new legislation?
