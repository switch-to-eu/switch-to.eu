**Summary:** Berlin, Bremen, Hamburg, Hessen, North Rhine-Westphalia and Rhineland-Palatinate agreed a framework contract for an open-source education platform without software from Microsoft or Google. It runs on Nextcloud software with IONOS infrastructure, is bought through the public IT cooperative govdigital, and runs for four years. Schools are not obliged to use it.

**Claims**
- `c1` Six German states (Berlin, Bremen, Hamburg, Hessen, North Rhine-Westphalia and Rhineland-Palatinate) agreed a framework contract for an open-source education platform that works without software from US tech companies such as Microsoft or Google.
- `c2` The agreement became known on the sidelines of the German federal government's Digital Summit in Berlin.
- `c3` Software from Nextcloud, a company based in Stuttgart, is the technical basis. The hosting provider IONOS supplies the infrastructure.
- `c4` Schools and school authorities get IONOS Nextcloud Workspace, a Microsoft 365 alternative that Nextcloud and IONOS presented in 2025. It covers file sharing, chat, video conferencing, email, calendar and office applications.
- `c5` Procurement runs through govdigital eG, the cooperative of German public IT service providers, so the states can buy the service without a new tender.
- `c6` The contract runs for four years at first. According to Nextcloud, the states' schools and school authorities are not obliged to use it.
- `c7` heise writes that the platform is meant as an alternative that meets GDPR requirements and protects against access by US authorities, such as access made possible by the US CLOUD Act.
- `c8` Nextcloud founder and managing director Frank Karlitschek told dpa that the public sector can act as a "lighthouse customer" and that Nextcloud doesn't need subsidies, only such customers.

**Quotes**
> "We need European champions to achieve digital sovereignty and strengthen the European software sector." (Frank Karlitschek to dpa, translated from German)
> "We don't need subsidies either, just lighthouse customers." (Frank Karlitschek to dpa, translated from German)
