**Summary:** netzpolitik.org (Tomas Rudl) on the Council's negotiating position on the data part of the Digital Omnibus, agreed on 21 October 2026: what changes in the GDPR and the ePrivacy rules, who voted against, and what comes next. Translated from German.

**Claims**
- `c1` EU member states agreed their negotiating position on the data part of the Digital Omnibus on Wednesday 21 October 2026, according to the Irish Council presidency.
- `c2` The European Parliament is expected to reach its position in February 2027. Trilogue negotiations start after that.
- `c3` Under the Council position, processing personal data to train AI would be allowed on the basis of legitimate interest, without pseudonymisation. The legal basis stays Article 6 GDPR, including the right to object.
- `c4` The Council does not formally change the definition of personal data. It adds pseudonymisation rules meant to ease AI training, with guidance from the European Data Protection Board. The Commission had proposed taking pseudonymised data out of the GDPR.
- `c5` The right of access would not apply to unfounded or excessive requests.
- `c6` The Council rejected the Commission's idea of a central browser setting for tracking consent. It keeps the ePrivacy rules with new exceptions where no consent is needed (service delivery, reach measurement, device security) and proposes that sites wait four months before asking again for cookie consent.
- `c7` Germany, Austria and Estonia voted against the Council position.
- `c8` Civil society groups called the package the biggest setback for digital fundamental rights, and noyb called the AI training provisions a "digital expropriation".
