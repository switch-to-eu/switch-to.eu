**Summary:** noyb's reaction to the Council's negotiating position on the GDPR part of the Commission's Digital Omnibus. noyb is an advocacy group: its reading of the text is attributed below. Its claim on pseudonymised data conflicts with [[2026-11-03-netzpolitik-council-data-omnibus#c4]]. The Council's own text was not read.

**Claims**
- `c1` noyb says EU member states approved, on a Wednesday, a Council text on the Commission's Digital Omnibus that amends the GDPR, published the following day.
- `c2` According to noyb, the Council text (Article 88bis, Article 88c in the Commission proposal) would presume that companies have an overriding legitimate interest when they train or use any AI product, so no consent would be needed.
- `c3` noyb says the provision would also cover data collected years ago, such as chats or social media posts, and people who were never customers of the AI company.
- `c4` noyb says the Council added references to the right to object (Article 21) and to Article 24 measures, and argues these apply to any processing anyway and add no real safeguard.
- `c5` noyb says member states proposed treating pseudonymised data as generally outside the GDPR.
- `c6` noyb says the Council text is a position, not law: it still has to be negotiated with the European Parliament, which has not adopted its position, and the Commission.
- `c7` noyb says the Commission put the proposal on a fast track without a fundamental rights assessment.
- `c8` Max Schrems calls the change a "digital expropriation" of Europeans and says it could make practices that are illegal today, such as personalised advertising without consent, legal when they involve AI.

**Quotes**
