**Summary:** heise summarises reporting by 404 Media, based on talks with eight current and one former Proton employee and on internal Slack messages and meeting recordings, that CEO Andy Yen pushed staff to use AI heavily for coding and dismissed security concerns harshly. Yen apologised on Reddit for his tone, said AI use will never come at the cost of security, promised to publish how Proton uses AI and reviews code, and cancelled planned re-interviews of staff. Not a breach of user data.

**Claims**
- `c1` heise reports that 404 Media spoke with eight current and one former Proton employee and reviewed internal Slack messages and meeting recordings.
- `c2` According to 404 Media's reporting, when an employee asked in the company's security Slack channel for code to be security-tested for a few days before release, CEO Andy Yen called this "nonsense and loser thinking" and said one can be fast and secure.
- `c3` According to 404 Media, an employee wrote that "security is currently a low priority" at Proton and that AI would not solve this.
- `c4` According to a meeting recording held by 404 Media, ninety percent of Proton's code in October 2026 was written by AI.
- `c5` Proton published an "AI manifesto" by its CTO shortly after the 404 Media report, which says that falling behind on AI is an existential threat to Proton's mission. heise notes the manifesto mentions security only once, to say it won't go into the topic.
- `c6` Andy Yen apologised for his communication style in a post on r/ProtonMail, said AI use at Proton would never come at the cost of security, and said Proton scans its own code with AI to find vulnerabilities.
- `c7` Yen promised to publish how Proton uses AI internally, to open-source several internal tools including extended versions of Lumo, and to publish how code and security reviews are done at Proton along with its AI guidelines.
- `c8` heise reports that some Proton employees were to be re-interviewed in early November about their willingness to follow the AI course. Yen withdrew this in his Reddit statement.
- `c9` heise reports that Proton, where AI use was first banned entirely, now allows it for a range of uses, reportedly including processing financial data and drafting replies to customers on Reddit and LinkedIn.

**Quotes**
> "I made mistakes, but I will learn from them." (Andy Yen on Reddit, as quoted by heise, translated from German)
