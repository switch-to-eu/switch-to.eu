**Summary:** The Council's 179-page negotiating mandate on the Digital Omnibus (file 2025/0360 (COD)), the primary text. Changes against the Commission proposal are marked in bold and deletions in strikethrough. Plain text extraction loses that marking, so the pages cited below were read as rendered images. This settles the conflict between [[2026-11-03-noyb-council-omnibus-ai-training#c5]] and [[2026-11-03-netzpolitik-council-data-omnibus#c4]]: the definition stays, and a new article takes pseudonymised data out of the GDPR for one group, third parties unable to identify the person. It is a negotiating position, not law.

**Claims**
- `c1` The Permanent Representatives Committee agreed the mandate at its meeting on 21 October 2026. The document is dated 22 October 2026 (p. 1).
- `c2` The Council deletes the Commission's added sentences on the definition of personal data in GDPR Article 4(1) (p. 131) and the matching explanation in recital 27 (p. 24).
- `c3` New Article 25a(2) says: "Pseudonymised data shall not be considered personal data for third party if that third party is unable to identify the natural person to whom the data relates." (p. 140)
- `c4` Limits on c3: data passed on to another third party that has means reasonably likely to identify the person is personal data (Article 25a(3)), data stays personal data for a processor acting for a controller (Article 25a(4), p. 140), and for a controller applying pseudonymisation the person remains identifiable, so the GDPR applies (recital 27b, p. 26).
- `c5` Recital 27c says controllers "remain fully responsible to determine and demonstrate whether pseudonymised data do not lead to re-identification" (p. 27). The European Data Protection Board is to issue an opinion on pseudonymisation and anonymisation, requested by its Chair no later than 6 months after entry into force (Article 25a(6) and (7), p. 141).
- `c6` New Article 88 bis(1) says: "The processing of personal data in the context of the development and operation of an AI system or of an AI model may be carried out for a legitimate interest of the controller or a third party in accordance with Article 6(1)(f) of this Regulation." (p. 145)
- `c7` Article 88 bis(3) says such processing, whatever the lawful ground, is subject to appropriate technical and organisational measures and safeguards, "including the right to object under Article 21" (p. 146).
- `c8` The Council deletes the Commission's wording "providing data subjects with an unconditional right to object" from the article (Commission Article 88c(2), p. 146) and recital 31, which also named an unconditional right to object (p. 29).
- `c9` New recital 33a says the development and operation of an AI system or model "does not in itself constitute a legitimate interest", and that applying Article 6(1)(f) to AI "is to be assessed on a case-by-case basis". It says the legitimate interest does not apply where overridden by the person's interests or rights, in particular for a child, or where other law explicitly requires consent (p. 32).
- `c10` On cookies, the Council deletes the Commission's Articles 88a and 88b, including machine-readable choice signals through browsers (pp. 143-145). Its ePrivacy text requires a single-click way to refuse, and after a refusal no new request for the same purpose for at least four months (p. 158).
