**Summary:** The European Commission told member states to pause draft national laws restricting minors' access to social media for 12 months while the EU-wide Kids Act is negotiated. Austria, the first country told to halt its draft law, objected. The Kids Act proposal would bar most under-13s from social media and restrict access for under-15s.

**Claims**
- `c1` On 2026-10-29 the Commission's spokesperson Thomas Regnier said the Commission "has decided to postpone for 12 months any draft national laws overlapping with the future harmonized rules" on social media access for minors.
- `c2` Austria was the first country told to halt its legislation. It had asked the Commission in July to check its draft law banning under-14s. The deadline for the Commission's answer lapsed on 2026-10-29 and the Commission told Austria to pause adoption for a year.
- `c3` Austrian State Secretary Alexander Pröll called it "unacceptable" that Brussels blocks national laws without a completed European solution. Vice-Chancellor Andreas Babler said Austria will keep preparing its law so it can apply as soon as the pause ends.
- `c4` The Commission proposed the Kids Act in September 2026. Politico says the proposal would bar access to social media for minors under 13 in most cases and severely restrict access for teens under 15.
- `c5` The Kids Act proposal also sets safety-by-design requirements for platforms, including video games and AI companions.
- `c6` Member states have to agree the Kids Act with the European Parliament and the Commission before it can apply.
- `c7` Greece has already passed its own law for under-15s. Politico says Greece will likely be able to keep it because it went through the process before the EU proposal.

**Quotes**
> "A European problem requires a united European response." (Thomas Regnier, Commission spokesperson)
