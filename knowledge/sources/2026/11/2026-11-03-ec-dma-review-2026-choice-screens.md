**Summary:** The Commission's staff working document for the first DMA review, published 2026-04-28. Used only for section 2.2.1 on defaults and choice screens (Article 6(3)). Figures on usage are ones the Commission cites from companies.

**Claims**
- `c1` Article 6(3) of the DMA requires gatekeepers to let users easily change default settings, including through choice screens for search engines, virtual assistants and web browsers, where those services are designated.
- `c2` The services subject to Article 6(3) are Apple's iOS, iPadOS and Safari; Alphabet's Android, Chrome and Google Search; and Microsoft's Windows.
- `c3` The Commission says Apple and Alphabet rolled out choice screens with randomised lists of alternative providers for browsers and search engines.
- `c4` The Commission says Microsoft complied by making more apps uninstallable and pinning the default browser to the taskbar; it does not mention a Microsoft search choice screen.
- `c5` The Commission opened a non-compliance investigation into Apple's choice screen design on 2024-03-25 and closed it after Apple made changes.
- `c6` The Commission cites Aloha reporting a 250% rise in new users, and Firefox daily active users doubling on iOS in Germany and France, after the choice screens.
- `c7` The Commission says it is premature to decide whether to expand Article 6(3), and will keep monitoring choice screens and AI chatbots with a search component.
