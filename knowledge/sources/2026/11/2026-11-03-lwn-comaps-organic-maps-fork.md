**Summary:** A guest article in LWN.net on the governance dispute around [[services/organic-maps]] and the CoMaps fork that followed. It sets out the Kayak referral links, the hidden server component, the open letter to the shareholders and the fork's first months. Allegations in the open letter are attributed, and LWN says some were not backed by sources.

**Claims**
- `c1` LWN reports that Organic Maps started in December 2020 as a community fork of the last open-source version of MAPS.ME, with Roman Tsisyk as the main driving force and former MAPS.ME developers Viktar Havaka and Alexander Borsuk as active contributors.
- `c2` In November 2023 (release 2023.11.17) an experimental "Details on Kayak" hotel button was merged that gave Organic Maps a referral bonus on bookings, and F-Droid flagged the app with an anti-feature label for ads.
- `c3` In December 2024 Tsisyk disclosed a server component used since 2021 to pick map download mirrors that had been kept out of public view, and said Borsuk had removed its MIT licence and enabled request logging; Borsuk did not dispute making the changes.
- `c4` An open letter dated 16 April 2025 to Havaka, Tsisyk and Borsuk asked for a governance structure that would stop Organic Maps from becoming a for-profit venture and for financial transparency.
- `c5` LWN notes that the letter's claims that Tsisyk tried to remove the other two from GitHub and that Borsuk used donations for a vacation came without sources.
- `c6` By the end of April 2025 contributors had copied the code to Codeberg as CoMaps and started taking donations on Open Collective, and the name was confirmed by a vote that ended on 20 May 2025.
- `c7` LWN reports that Organic Maps OÜ was owned by Havaka and Tsisyk and that its public annual reports hold only basic information and a balance sheet.
- `c8` LWN expected Organic Maps and CoMaps to coexist as separate apps in the app stores, since negotiations appeared to be at an impasse.
