**Summary:** The joint position paper behind the Rapporteur story, dated November 2026 and coordinated by the Modal Foundation. It asks for a "personal data server" (PDS) built to one common standard for every social media account, with the AT Protocol's PDS as the starting point, through the Digital Fairness Act and through enforcement of the Digital Markets Act. It includes the coalition's own test of exporting followers from LinkedIn, Instagram and X. The page shows the signatory list as last updated 3 November 2026. The publication date above is that update date.

**Claims**
- `c1` On 2026-11-03 the page listed 70 signatories: 45 builders and 20 organisations from 15 countries. The paper is coordinated by the Modal Foundation (Stichting Modal). Signatories sign in a personal capacity.
- `c2` The coalition says the GDPR and the Digital Markets Act already require platforms to hand over people's data, but in formats other services can't easily use, and neither law requires platforms to hand over people's connections in a form that works elsewhere.
- `c3` In its own test in September 2026, the coalition counted 9 steps to export data from LinkedIn, 16 from Instagram and 10 from X. Followers arrived as a CSV of names linking back to LinkedIn, a list of usernames and account IDs from Instagram, and a list of numeric IDs without usernames from X. In none of the three cases could followers move with the user.
- `c4` The core ask: every social media platform should hold its users' data, connections and interactions in a personal data server that follows one common standard, so any app the user authorises can read and write to the same account, and the user can move the server to another host.
- `c5` The coalition wants the Digital Fairness Act, which it says is expected as a proposal in Q4 2026, to establish a right to take your network with you, with the AT Protocol's PDS as the starting point for the standard.
- `c6` It asks that leaving a platform be no harder than joining it, for every platform, and that obstructive export flows count as an unfair practice.
- `c7` It asks to add the removal or suppression of links pointing to a person's other accounts to Annex I of the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, and to bar restrictions on user-requested contact matching.
- `c8` Under the Digital Markets Act, it asks the Commission to make Article 6(9) data portability work now, defined as live sync of content and connections, and to examine whether Meta surfacing Threads content inside the Instagram feed is compatible with Article 6(5).
- `c9` The coalition says the network of apps built on the AT Protocol has more than 45 million users, that people use more than a thousand apps on the protocol every week, and that the AT Protocol PDS is a draft standard within the IETF.
- `c10` The signatory list includes a builder from Bluesky PBC (United States) alongside builders from European projects such as Eurosky. The site links a supporting note titled "why AT Protocol can stand independently of Bluesky".
