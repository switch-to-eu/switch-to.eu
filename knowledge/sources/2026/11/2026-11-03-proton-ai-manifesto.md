**Summary:** Opinion post on Proton's blog by CTO Bart Butler, published three days after the 404 Media investigation. It republishes in full an internal memo arguing that engineers' work is moving from writing code to directing AI models. It gives no figure for how much of Proton's code AI writes. Read in full.

**Claims**
- `c1` Proton published the post on 1 November 2026. Butler says he wrote the internal memo about a month earlier, and the post carries it in full.
- `c2` Butler's introduction says the memo is not an in-depth engineering document and "does not delve into the specifics of security, reliability, or infrastructure."
- `c3` Butler's introduction describes the memo as the AI adoption path Proton must follow "to remain competitive and secure".
- `c4` The memo says of AI: "we are being left behind here, and it is an existential threat to the success of the mission itself."
- `c5` The memo lists code reviews among possible bottlenecks and adds: "yes, we need to and will get to a point where most human code review will end".
- `c6` The memo says "Documentation and tests remain crucial", while best practices may change as costs change.
- `c7` The memo says engineers are accountable "in a way a model can never be", and that AI needs to work "with less and less human babysitting".

**Quotes**
> "yes, we need to and will get to a point where most human code review will end" (Bart Butler, Proton CTO, published 1 November 2026)
