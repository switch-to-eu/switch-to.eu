**Summary:** More than 35 builders of alternative social apps signed a joint statement asking the European Commission to use the Digital Fairness Act to give users a right to take their social network with them when they leave a platform. They propose a common "personal data server" standard for every platform and a ban on suppressing links to rival services. Rapporteur reports the Commission is due to present the Digital Fairness Act on 11 November 2026.

**Claims**
- `c1` More than 35 social app builders signed a joint statement, published at digitalfreedomofmovement.eu, urging the European Commission to use the upcoming Digital Fairness Act (DFA) to give users of large platforms a right to take their social network with them when they switch platforms.
- `c2` Signatories include Sherif Elsayed-Ali, co-lead of the alternative social network Eurosky, and Robin Berjon of the IPFS Project.
- `c3` The signatories say the data downloads that mainstream platforms offer today aren't useful, and want interoperability rules applied to every platform.
- `c4` They propose that every platform holds each user's data, connections and interactions in a "personal data server" built to one common standard, so people can use any app and keep their data and network. Portable assets would cover the user's identity, their content in a usable format and their connections.
- `c5` Rapporteur reports the Commission is due to present the DFA, the EU's planned revamp of online consumer law, on 11 November 2026.
- `c6` The signatories argue that being unable to leave a social app without losing your network is a consumer harm comparable to deceptive design.
- `c7` They want practices such as removing or suppressing links to profiles on alternative services banned under Annex I of the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, which Rapporteur says is widely expected to be changed alongside the DFA.
- `c8` The signatories point out that the interoperability rules of the Digital Markets Act don't cover all leading social media platforms, such as X, so X's ban on "account matching" features (which help users find people they follow on other platforms) falls outside EU enforcement.

**Quotes**
> "Every platform should hold its users' data, connections and interactions in a personal data server built to one common standard, so people can use whichever app they choose and keep their data and network." (joint statement, quoted by Rapporteur)
