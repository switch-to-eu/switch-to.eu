## Current state
Article 6(3) requires gatekeepers to let users change defaults easily, including through choice screens for browsers and search engines. [[2026-11-03-ec-dma-review-2026-choice-screens#c1]] The Commission's first review, published on 2026-04-28, found it premature to expand that article. [[2026-11-03-ec-dma-review-2026-choice-screens#c7]] A coalition of social app builders says the DMA and the GDPR require platforms to hand over data but not people's connections in a usable form, and that its interoperability rules don't cover all leading social platforms, such as X. [[2026-11-03-dfom-joint-position-paper#c2]] [[2026-11-03-euractiv-dfa-social-media-switching#c8]]

## Facts
- 2026-11-03 · A coalition of social app builders asked the Commission to make the DMA's Article 6(9) data portability work as live sync of content and connections, and to examine whether Meta surfacing Threads content in the Instagram feed is compatible with Article 6(5). [[2026-11-03-dfom-joint-position-paper#c8]]
- 2026-11-03 · The same coalition points out that the DMA's interoperability rules don't cover all leading social platforms, such as X, so X's ban on "account matching" features falls outside EU enforcement. [[2026-11-03-euractiv-dfa-social-media-switching#c8]]
- 2026-04-28 · The Commission's first DMA review said it is premature to decide whether to expand Article 6(3) on defaults and choice screens. [[2026-11-03-ec-dma-review-2026-choice-screens#c7]]
